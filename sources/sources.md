# Research Sources

This document presents the seven sources selected to build the AI-powered knowledge base about **Grace Hopper and the Foundations of Modern Programming**.

The research combines journalistic articles, educational videos, academic content, and encyclopedic references to investigate Grace Hopper's biography, the history of computing, compiler development, COBOL, and her influence on modern programming.

## 1. Source Selection Criteria

The sources were selected according to the following criteria:

- **Relevance:** Direct connection to Grace Hopper's life, career, and technological contributions.
- **Credibility:** Preference for recognized publications, educational institutions, and specialized educational channels.
- **Historical Context:** Information about early computers, programming languages, and computing history.
- **Technical Value:** Explanations of compilers, FLOW-MATIC, COBOL, and programming language evolution.
- **Complementarity:** Different perspectives and levels of detail to support a broader understanding of the subject.

## 4. Source Diversity and Limitations

The research combines different types of material:

- **Journalistic articles:** Provide accessible explanations and historical overviews.
- **Educational videos:** Offer narrative explanations and additional historical context.
- **Academic and institutional material:** Contribute educational and historical perspectives.
- **Encyclopedic reference:** Provides a broader chronology and identifies additional historical references.

Some sources cover the same events, especially the A-0 compiler, FLOW-MATIC, COBOL, and the moth incident. This overlap is useful for comparing accounts, but repeated information should not automatically be treated as independent confirmation.

The research also recognizes that some technological developments involved teams and institutions. Individual contributions must therefore be distinguished from collective achievements.
