# Grace Hopper — Second Brain with AI

An AI-powered knowledge notebook dedicated to studying Grace Hopper, her contributions to computer science, and her influence on the evolution of modern programming.

This project was developed as part of a DIO challenge, using Google Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) to create a second brain based on selected research sources.

## Objective

Build an AI-powered knowledge base to investigate Grace Hopper's life, academic background, military career, and technological contributions, understanding how her work helped transform programming from a hardware-oriented activity into a more accessible and human-readable discipline.

## About the Project

The notebook works as a personal study assistant, using selected articles and educational videos to answer questions, explain historical and technical concepts, and generate learning materials.

The research focuses on:

- Grace Hopper's biography and academic career.
- Early computers and the Harvard Mark I.
- Development of the A-0 compiler.
- FLOW-MATIC and the evolution of programming languages.
- The creation and historical importance of COBOL.
- Software standardization and accessibility.
- Grace Hopper's legacy in modern computing.

## Research Sources

Seven sources were selected, combining journalistic articles, educational videos, academic content, and encyclopedic references.

The selection considered relevance, credibility, historical context, technical information, and complementary perspectives.

The complete source catalog, including links and selection criteria, is available in:

[`sources/sources.md`](sources/sources.md)

## AI Notebook

**Tool:** Google Gemini Notebook

The notebook was configured to act as a specialist in Grace Hopper and the history of programming.

Its instructions include:

- Answer questions based on the selected sources.
- Provide citations supporting its answers.
- Explain historical and technical concepts.
- Connect historical contributions with the evolution of programming.
- Distinguish documented facts from interpretations.
- Acknowledge limitations when the sources do not provide enough information.
